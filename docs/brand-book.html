Start here

Three rules that carry the rest.

If you remember nothing else from this book, these are the decisions everything else hangs off — and the ones that are most obvious when they are broken.

The page is never pure white

The ground is ivory, #FAFAF7. Cards are pure white, and they are the only pure white anywhere. That contrast — white sheets on an ivory ground — is what makes the surface read as paper rather than as a screen.

One hot colour, used sparingly

Terracotta is the single accent. Everything around it is warm and quiet so it lands every time it appears. Two accents on one page means neither is the accent.

Warm greys, never neutral ones

Every grey has warmth in it. A neutral grey on ivory reads as dirty — which is the single fastest way to make work look like it was not made for skeo.

Colour

Two palettes, one system.

Day is the default and the one to use for anything printed or exported. Night exists for the website's dark mode. They are not two brands — the same roles, filled differently, which is why the token names are identical in both.

Typography

Manrope, with DM Mono for the labels.

Manrope is the brand face and carries everything — every tracking value in the system is tuned to its widths, and headings depend on its 800 weight. DM Mono does one job: uppercase labels and figures. Nothing else on the page is monospaced.

Rhythm

Six bands, and no two neighbours alike.

Every section owns a background, and no two adjacent sections share one. That is what makes a long page read as chapters rather than as one scroll with three dark slabs dropped into it. The palette is warm throughout, so bands separate by weight and pigment rather than by temperature.

Paper — #FAFAF7the default reading surface
Soft — #F0EEE6analytical sections: comparison, pricing
Alt (manilla) — #EFE6D2human sections: the problem, the reviews
Tint (book cloth) — #F0E1D6the brand moment — the only light band carrying the accent's hue
Inkopens and closes the page
Deep (book cloth)the two product sections · highlight is manilla, not a bright accent

The two dark bands are lit from the top edge rather than flat-filled, so a full-bleed band reads as a surface with a light on it. They are two characters, not two shades.

Form

Corners, shadow and the shapes they make.

Border, fill, radius and shadow each say "separate object". Spend them by role — lift the one thing that needs lifting, rather than stamping one radius and one shadow on every block, which flattens the hierarchy until nothing leads.

Radius scale

8 · 12 · 16 · 22 · 28 · 999 — small controls take the small end, cards the large. Pick by the size of the thing, not by habit.

Elevation

Two parts each: a tight contact shadow plus a wide ambient one, tinted rather than neutral black. Neutral shadow on a warm ground reads as grime.

For the playbooks

Taking this into a printed book.

A page and a PDF are not the same object, and a few things have to change on the way across. These are the ones that go wrong most often.

Use Day, always

Night is a screen palette. Charcoal grounds drink ink, cost a fortune to print and look grey rather than black. Every book, deck and PDF is set in Day.

Ivory is the page, not white

Set the page ground to #FAFAF7 even on paper. If the run is uncoated stock that already sits warm, drop the tint and let the paper do it — do not print ivory onto ivory.

Body copy sits at ink-2

Not full ink. #4A4741 at 10–11pt is the printed equivalent of the site's body copy; full black at body size reads harsher on paper than it does on screen.

Keep the label face

DM Mono at ~8pt, uppercase, +0.11em, for running heads, figure numbers and captions. It is the most recognisable thing in the system after the wordmark.

Heading tracking is not optional

Manrope 800 at −0.03em. Left at default tracking, a display heading loses the tightness that makes it skeo's rather than any other deck's.

One accent per spread

Terracotta earns its place by being rare. A spread with four terracotta elements has no accent — it has a colour scheme.

Mind the deep band in CMYK

Book cloth #4E2A1E is a brown, and it must stay a brown. Converted carelessly it drifts to a muddy near-black and takes the whole warm story with it.

Margins with air

The site runs generous gutters and so should the page: nothing tighter than 18mm, and let a heading have its own white space rather than filling the spread.

Voice

How skeo writes.

The words are design material too. The tone is plain, specific and slightly understated — it never oversells, because the whole proposition is proof rather than promises.

Do

✓Write skeo in lowercase, always — including at the start of a sentence.
✓Say the concrete thing. "Twelve lessons, four projects" beats "comprehensive curriculum".
✓Use sentence case for headings. Title Case Reads As A Different Brand.
✓Admit limits plainly. "No platform can promise that, and we won't pretend otherwise."
✓Let a full stop end a headline. "Everything you need. In one place."

Don't

✕Capitalise Skeo, or set the wordmark in anything but Manrope 800.
✕Reach for "revolutionary", "unlock your potential", "game-changing".
✕Put a number on the page that nothing behind it supports.
✕Use emoji as section markers, or exclamation marks in body copy.
✕Stretch, recolour, outline or add a shadow to the wordmark.
